The journey

From a lab result to first contracts, in six steps.

Pick a step to see what happens and where it happens.

Discover

Lab audits and technology scouting. We look for research that could solve someone's problem, inside the institutions of a StartNet Lab.

Where it happens: StartNet Labs →

Validate

A 7-week sprint with 100 customer interviews. You test whether your research solves a problem someone will pay for.

Where it happens: Scholar Founders Fellowship →

Build

A proof of concept with a GCC or industry partner. The partner brings the problem, funds the proof of concept and can become the first customer.

Where it happens: Problem statements →

Form

Company, IP licence and standard terms. One set of terms, agreed once per institution, not company by company.

Where it happens: Standard terms →

Fund

Grants, angels and research funds. Lab members get help with grants, and lab teams demo to investors at the Lab-to-Market Summit.

Where it happens: Lab-to-Market Summit →

Scale

Pilots and first contracts, with the GCCs, industry partners and government bodies who set the problems in the first place.

Questions

Questions scholars, faculty and institutions ask first.

Who can join a Lab?

PhD scholars, post-docs and faculty in the field. Final-year master's students join by invitation.

What do I get?

A place in the Scholar Founders Fellowship, mentors from industry, a funded proof of concept if selected, help with grants, and standard terms if you form a company.

What does the Fellowship cost?

The fee is [placeholder]. Seats are mostly sponsor- or grant-funded.

How much of the company do founders keep?

A clear majority. In the illustrative split, founders hold 78% at formation, with a 10% ESOP pool. The split is a draft and subject to legal review. Try the split.

Will my university allow it?

Faculty can use leave or sabbatical provisions under AICTE's National Innovation and Startup Policy, where their institution has adopted it. Institutions that join the Institution Partner Programme get a policy kit and IP committee set-up aligned with that policy.

Who owns the IP?

The institution provides IP, lab access and a host incubator, and takes a small stake and/or royalty. Final terms follow each institution's IP policy.

Can I apply to the Fellowship as faculty?

Yes. The Scholar Founders Fellowship is a 7-week customer-discovery sprint for scholars and faculty.

What does the Institution Partner Programme include?

Membership of a StartNet Lab, a policy kit and IP committee set-up aligned with AICTE's innovation and startup policy, faculty development, AI-readiness services, and a Campus Innovation Index score.

What does it cost?

Indicatively from ₹5 L a year, by size. This is an estimate [C] until the rate card is published.

Do we negotiate terms for every company?

No. We publish one set of terms and agree them once per institution, not company by company.

What happens if a Lab doesn't produce companies?

No company formed in 24 months means the Lab closes or pivots, and we publish the decision.

What does an industry partner do?

The GCC or industry partner brings the problem, funds the proof of concept and can become the first customer. It takes equity only if it invests cash.

What do GCCs get?

Problem-solving research and access to IP and talent.

How do we post a problem statement?

Each problem statement is co-signed by an industry, GCC or government sponsor. Post a problem statement on StartNet X.
