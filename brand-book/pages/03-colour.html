03.2

How the palette is derived

OKLCH hue
One axis,
two directions
SNX Red · 25°the given
Marigold · 70°analogous, +45°
Ink · Lagoon · 205°complement, 180° across
  1. 01Secondary: take the complement to near-black.Ink #0C1719 sits 180° across the wheel at lightness 0.20. It reads as black, yet its blue-green bias makes the red look warmer and brighter beside it.
  2. 02Tertiary, cool: lift the same complement.Lagoon #117780 is Ink's lighter sibling (lightness 0.52), so the cool tertiary is part of the same family rather than a new hue.
  3. 03Tertiary, warm: step 45° toward yellow.Marigold #FBA62A is the red's analogous neighbour at high lightness: warm and celebratory, the colour of a marigold garland.
Considered and rejected

Blue-violet, 265°. Too close to Research and the data blue.

Rose, 0°. Reads as Women and muddies the red.

Green, 145°. Collides with Connect and "success".

Confidence tags on every figure work without colour: solid, outline, dashed.

  1. 01Functional colour lives in product, forms, dashboards and data. Never in marketing layouts or near the logo.
  2. 02Error shares the brand's red hue, so it always carries an icon, the word "Error" and a 2 px left rule. Never fill a whole form red.
  3. 03Marigold and Lagoon never signal status. Warning stays amber with its icon so the two never read as the same signal.
  4. 04Every state pairs colour with an icon and a word. Colour alone is not an accessible state.

Gradients and texture

Ember on Ink

The signature. Long ink hold, red arriving in the last third. Covers and stage backdrops.

Halo

A glow centred on a full stop. The only permitted glow, and only on ink.

Nightfall

Ink through its own ramp. Depth on dark grounds where a flat field feels heavy.

Pulli texture

The dot grid at 6–14% Paper on ink. The brand's only pattern.

Paper warm

Barely there. Gives a light page a direction of light without becoming a colour.

03.10

Proportion and contrast

A working ratio, not a law: about 60 parts Paper, 25 Ink, up to 8 Red, up to 5 tertiary and the brand accent only on its own brand. Red earns its force from scarcity: one full stop, one action, one number per surface.
